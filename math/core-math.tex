\section*{Core Mathematical Framework}

Triangle Mutiny begins with a minimal set of geometric primitives that define
the structure of reasoning manifolds, operator interactions, and drift behavior.
These primitives form the backbone for collapse geometry, spectral mismatch,
modal adjacency, and stability envelopes.

\subsection*{1. Reasoning Manifold}

Let \( M \) denote the latent reasoning manifold. Points \( p \in M \) represent
semantic or operator states. No assumptions are made about global topology;
only local geometric structure is required.

\subsection*{2. Curvature Magnitude}

Each point \( p \in M \) has an associated curvature magnitude:
\[
K : M \rightarrow \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}.
\]

Curvature measures the bending of reasoning trajectories. High curvature
indicates sharp conceptual turns; low curvature indicates stable reasoning flow.

\subsection*{3. Similarity Relation}

For reasoning components \( A, B \in T_pM \), define the similarity relation:
\[
\Phi(A,B) = \frac{\langle A, B \rangle}{\|A\| \cdot \|B\|}.
\]

This cosine-style similarity determines alignment between components and is
used in curvature composition and spectral mismatch.

\subsection*{4. Drift Fields}

A drift field is a smooth vector field:
\[
X \in \Gamma(TM).
\]

Drift fields describe directional tendencies of reasoning. They are used to
define Lie brackets, holonomy drift, and stability envelopes.

\subsection*{5. Operator Fields}

An operator field is a map:
\[
\nabla : TM \rightarrow \mathrm{End}(TM).
\]

Operator fields encode how drift fields interact and transform. They define
the structural rules of motion within the manifold.

\subsection*{6. Lie Bracket}

For drift fields \( X, Y \), the Lie bracket is:
\[
[X, Y] = XY - YX.
\]

The Lie bracket measures non-commutativity of drift flows and is central to
detecting operator collapse and Jacobi identity violations.

\subsection*{7. Curvature Composition (Constructive Form)}

Curvature cannot be mixed linearly. The only stable composition is:
\[
K_{A \oplus B} =
\sqrt{K_A^2 + K_B^2 + 2 K_A K_B \Phi(A,B)}.
\]

This operator is used throughout Triangle Mutiny to prevent curvature
discontinuities and collapse surfaces.

\subsection*{8. Spectral Structure}

Each operator field induces a local linear operator:
\[
L(p) : T_pM \rightarrow T_pM.
\]

The spectrum of \( L(p) \) is:
\[
\mathrm{Spec}(L(p)) = \{\lambda_1, \ldots, \lambda_n\}.
\]

Spectral signatures are used to detect mismatch, drift, and collapse.

\subsection*{9. Modal Labeling}

Each point \( p \in M \) has a modal label:
\[
\mu(p) \in L,
\]
where \( L \) is a finite set of semantic modes. Modal adjacency constraints
are defined in a separate module.

\subsection*{10. Holonomy Drift (Primitive Form)}

Given a path \( \gamma \), define primitive holonomy drift:
\[
H(\gamma) = \sum_{i} \Delta_{\mathrm{spec}}(p_i)
           + \Delta_{\mathrm{curv}}(p_i)
           + \Delta_{\mathrm{modal}}(p_i).
\]

This primitive form is refined in the stability-envelope module.

\subsection*{11. Stability Primitives}

A point is stable when:
\[
K(p) \text{ is continuous}, \quad
\mathrm{Spec}(L(p)) \text{ is bounded}, \quad
\mu(p) \text{ respects adjacency}.
\]

These primitives form the basis of the full stability envelope.

\section*{Summary}

This file defines the minimal geometric objects required for all downstream
Triangle Mutiny modules. No collapse geometry, spectral mismatch, modal
adjacency, or stability envelopes are defined here; those appear in their
respective files and depend on the primitives introduced above.
